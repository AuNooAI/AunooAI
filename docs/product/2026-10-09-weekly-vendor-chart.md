# The Agentic SOC Top 40 Movers, and a weekly post about the market
_2026-10-09 · aisocnews.com front page; a weekly LinkedIn post for the publisher_

## What shipped
- **A weekly chart.** The Agentic SOC Top 40 Movers now sits at the top of the aisocnews.com
  front page. It ranks AI SOC vendors by momentum.
- **What each row shows:**
  - the vendor's move since last week, or a New or Re-entry marker;
  - last week's place and its best place so far;
  - a small line showing its momentum week by week;
  - its stage and speed on the Market Maturity Map, and what most lifted its score.
- **A weekly LinkedIn post, ready to paste.** Every Monday the publisher gets an email with a
  draft post. It says what the market's vendors did the week before and what moved on the
  chart, and it lists the source of every claim.

## Why it matters
**Readers had no quick answer to "who is moving in this market?"** The front page had lists of
news and activity, each with its own measure. None gave one ranking that changes week to week.

**The chart uses ratings we already publish.** A vendor's place is its momentum score on the
Market Maturity Map. That score combines six measures over the last 90 days:
- headcount change;
- launches and partnerships;
- open roles;
- earned mentions;
- Crunchbase growth;
- the vendor's own posts.

The map prints its weights, so a reader can check why a vendor sits where it does.

**We tried simpler rankings first.**
- **Press coverage alone:** only 7 to 20 vendors a week had any, so the chart would have been
  half empty.
- **Counting developments:** most vendors had exactly one a week, so the order would have come
  down to the name.
- **Momentum:** it fills 40 places every week, and a typical vendor moves 1 to 3 places a week.

**The trend line shows direction, not just position.** Exaforce, now #1, rose from 64 to 84
over five weeks. 7AI, now #2, slipped from 88 to 83. All lines share one scale, so a reader can
compare both level and direction.

**Each week is fixed once it closes.** We may change the map's weights later. A past week's
chart, and each vendor's weeks at #1, will not change with them.

**The weekly post saves the publisher an afternoon and keeps the facts straight.**
- Last week's post was written by hand from the data.
- Now an AI model drafts the developments from the week's collected posts and coverage.
- The software checks each sentence against its source. It drops any sentence where the
  model put one vendor's news under another vendor's name. In testing, that check caught the
  model attributing Exaforce's CrowdStrike Marketplace listing to Exabeam.
- The figures in the post are written straight from the data, not by the model: the chart
  movers, the open-role count and the acquisitions line.

Who feels it:
- **Buyers** get a short weekly list of who is moving and what they shipped.
- **Vendors** get a public place to point to, and a reason to check back.
- **The publisher** gets a sourced draft every Monday instead of a blank page.

## Positioning notes
A market site lives on return visits, and a ranking that changes every week gives people a
reason to come back. Analyst quadrants appear once a year and hide their inputs. This chart
updates weekly and shows them. It ranks momentum, not quality, and the subtitle says so.

## Limits and what's next
- **It measures momentum, not who is best.** A large vendor growing slowly ranks below a small
  one hiring fast.
- **It moves slowly.** The score covers 90 days, so one announcement lifts a vendor over
  several weeks rather than overnight.
- **Only vendors the map can rate appear.** A vendor missing a required reading, such as
  headcount or funding, cannot chart.
- **History starts on 31 Aug 2026.** We dropped the first week, 24 Aug, because the map rated
  only 40 vendors then, against about 90 since, so its scores weren't comparable.
- **The post does not go to LinkedIn by itself.** The publisher tags the vendors and pastes it.
- **The closing summary line is sometimes missing.** The software drops it whenever the model
  mentions something the bullets don't. That happened in this week's draft.
- **The open-role trend starts next week.** It needs one stored week to compare against.
- **Only aisocnews.com has the post.** EV Battery Cells, on the same site, shows the chart with
  13 vendors but has the post switched off.
