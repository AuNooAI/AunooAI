# The site reads like a person wrote it, and Tuskira joins the coverage
_2026-09-05 · aisocnews.com — about page, front-page story cards, vendor registry_

## What shipped

- The about page was rewritten by hand. Same facts, same legal commitments,
  but sentences a person would actually write.
- One policy change on that page: people who appear in coverage can ask us to
  correct a mistake, but not to be removed. The material is public and the
  editorial choice of who appears is ours. Deleting what someone typed into
  our own forms is still offered, because that data is ours to delete.
- Story cards on the front page now always name the vendor in the headline.
  Before, a vendor's case study about a big customer could read as the
  customer's announcement.
- Story-card summaries no longer quote the marketing warm-up. The card now
  starts at the facts: numbers, dates, what happened.
- Tuskira is now covered: profile, funding, posts and hiring collected, and
  the company is placed on the Market Maturity Map.

## Why it matters

**Readers judge the site by its prose.** The about page is where a skeptical
reader goes to decide whether the site is credible, and ours read like machine
output — short identical sentences, verdict fragments, a too-clever closing
line. A reader who smells generated text on the about page assumes the rest
is generated too. The page now reads plainly, and a scan with our own
AI-tells detector finds nothing to flag on it.

**The DXC card showed both card problems at once.** 7AI published a case
study about DXC Technology. Our card led with DXC's name twice and 7AI's not
at all, and the summary opened with the post's own warm-up — "What does
agentic security look like at global scale? Ask DXC Technology. … The real
story is what came next." Readers got a riddle where they expected a fact.
The same card now reads "7ai: DXC went from proving the model…" and the
summary starts at "Across 25 delivery centers processing 4.5 million threats
a day…". The fix applies to every card, not just this one, and it never
touches quoted posts elsewhere on the site — a quotation stays the author's
own words.

**Tuskira** (San Francisco, founded 2024, $28.5M Series A co-led by Intel
Capital and SYN Ventures) was requested and is now tracked like any
established vendor: 47 employees and 3,817 followers on LinkedIn, 25 recent
company posts, funding profile on file, and a first placement on the map in
the Innovators tier — mid-field on size, quiet on momentum until its
coverage accumulates.

## Positioning notes

The page competes with newsletters written by people. Machine-flavoured
prose is the fastest way to lose that comparison, and the fixes here are all
of one kind: the site's own voice is edited, sources are quoted untouched.
That line — edit our words, never theirs — is worth repeating to anyone who
asks how an AI-assembled page can be trusted.

## Limits and what's next

- The summary filter is rule-based. It removes the shapes we have seen
  (rhetorical openers, drumroll pivots, short fragments, promotional
  vocabulary); a new shape of filler will get through until a rule is added.
- The DXC card is tagged "Customer". DXC also resells the product as its own
  service, which is arguably the bigger half of the story; the review
  taxonomy would call that a partnership. Not changed — open question for
  the operator.
- Tuskira has no earned press in the corpus yet, so its momentum score
  starts low. That corrects itself as coverage arrives; no action needed.
- Torq's website blocks our fetcher, so Tuskira-style direct site watching
  works for Tuskira but still not for Torq.
