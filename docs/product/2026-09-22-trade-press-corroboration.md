# The market report can now tell "the vendor says so" from "someone else checked"
_2026-09-22 · Market Monitor — the developments timeline and the corroboration label on every item_

## What shipped
Market Monitor now reads trade-press coverage of the companies it tracks, not just what those
companies publish about themselves.

Two things follow from that. When a publication reports something a vendor had already
announced, the report says so, and the item stops resting on the vendor's word alone. When a
publication reports something no vendor announced, the development appears at all — it used to
be invisible.

## Why it matters
Until today the timeline was built almost entirely from vendor LinkedIn posts, vendor websites
and vendor job adverts. That is one voice, and the report labelled it honestly: of the 940
developments we had ever recorded, 938 carried "vendor sources only".

A buyer reading that cannot tell an announcement anyone checked from one nobody did. The
distinction is the product. A funding round two outlets reported and a launch only the vendor
mentioned are different kinds of fact, and a timeline that renders them identically has thrown
away the thing that separates them.

**What changed, measured on the AI-in-the-SOC market.** We read 26 trade articles. Four
developments moved off the vendor's word onto an outside source. Three developments appeared
that we had never seen, including an acquisition — Cribl buying Radiant Security — and a $26M
funding round at Method Security. Acquisitions and funding rounds are the most consequential
things that happen in a market, and neither of those reached us through a vendor's own channel.

**The report will not be fooled by a press release.** GlobeNewswire, PR Newswire and the rest
distribute a company's own announcement for a fee. Counting one as outside confirmation would
let any vendor buy its way to "independently reported". Four of the articles we read this run
were wire releases. They were recorded, and they raised nothing.

**It also will not invent agreement.** A vendor can ship two products a fortnight apart. Linking
the coverage of the second to the first would produce a corroboration that never happened, which
is worse than missing one. The rule requires the article and the development to share something
specific — a product name, a sum of money, a named partner — and the company's own name does not
count, because every article about a company contains it.

## Release notes (copy-ready)
- Market Monitor now reads trade-press coverage, not only vendor announcements.
- A development reported by an outside publication is labelled as such, instead of resting on
  the vendor's own word.
- Developments that only the press reported now appear in the timeline. On the AI-SOC market
  this surfaced an acquisition and a funding round we had not previously recorded.
- Press releases distributed over a paid wire are recorded but do not count as outside
  confirmation.

## Demo / walkthrough
Open the market report and look at any item in the developments list. Each carries a provenance
label: "Vendor sources only", "Also reported independently", or "Reported by multiple
independent sources". The second and third are what this work makes reachable.

## Positioning notes
This is the substance behind the claim that we report what we observed rather than reprinting a
feed. Anyone can collect vendor announcements. Saying which of them anybody else confirmed, and
refusing to count a paid wire as confirmation, is the part that is hard and the part a buyer
should test us on.

It also answers the standing objection — "how is this not just vendor press releases?" — with a
number rather than a promise. Today most of it still is, the report says so on every row, and
that is now a measurement we can move.

## Limits and what's next
**Most developments still rest on the vendor's word.** 926 of 934. Today's work changed the
ceiling, not the average.

**The two suppressed report highlights stay suppressed.** Yesterday we withheld two highlights
because they only reflected what we had read. The share of developments with an outside source
moved from 11.9% to 11.8% — statistically nothing — against a threshold of 35%. The extractor is
working and using everything available; there simply is not much.

**The real constraint is now collection, not analysis.** This market's corpus holds about 250
trade-press articles in total, roughly 30 a month, against about 100 developments a month across
97 vendors. Reading more of the trade press is the next piece of work, and it is a collection
problem — more sources, better vendor-name matching at ingest — not another model.

**Nothing has run on the other customer sites yet.** Oviva and Sunstar have the code; neither has
processed its trade press. Sunstar does not have the events timeline switched on at all.

**Sales should not describe the report as independently corroborated.** Eight developments are.
The rest are not, and each row says which it is.
