# Daily Briefing: dates now mean what happened, not when we saw it
_2026-09-14 · Briefing Desk, incident detection, emerging topics_

## What shipped

- A briefing dates an event by what the article says happened and when. When the
  article does not say, the briefing writes "reported on" and the article's date, instead
  of presenting the article's date as the event date.
- Studies, guidelines and reports are dated by their own release. A press release in
  September about a paper published in May no longer reads as "research published this
  week".
- Emerging-topic timelines list only developments about that topic, each dated the same
  way. They no longer fill up with unrelated items that happened to be collected on the
  same days.
- Incidents name only the organisations the sources name. The briefing's own company and
  its competitors are no longer attached to items that never mention them.

## Why it matters

A reader checked one day's briefing for Sunstar and found three items whose dates were
the day we collected the article rather than the day the thing happened, including a
2024 guideline presented as released that week. A wrong date turns a true fact into a
false one, and a reader who catches one stops trusting the rest.

## What did not change

Incidents and emerging topics written before today keep their stored text until they are
regenerated. The Executive Briefing feature is separate and unchanged.

## Where it is live

bugfixing, sunstar, wiley and wileytest, deployed 14 September 2026.
