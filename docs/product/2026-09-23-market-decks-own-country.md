# Market decks now cite only their own country's press
_2026-09-23 · Forecast Tracker market decks and the Topics list, Sunstar customer site_

## What shipped
- Each country deck in the Forecast Tracker now draws its evidence only from publishers based in
  that country.
- The UK market has UK sources again: The Guardian's dentistry coverage, Dentistry.co.uk and the
  British Dental Journal.
- The oral-health topics in the Topics list now follow one naming pattern. The name shows the
  subject, the kind of source and the market.

## Why it matters

**Country decks were not really country decks.** Each Sunstar market deck, such as "Oral-Systemic
Health – United States", was meant to reflect that country's press. A rule to enforce this
existed, but it never switched on, so every market deck drew on whatever English-language (or
Japanese, or German) coverage came in. The US deck is the clearest case. Only 527 of the 1,406
articles in the US topic came from US publishers; the rest included UK, Indian, Canadian and
Australian outlets. Of the 44 strongest articles the deck could cite, 14 are left once we apply
the country rule and our usual removal of duplicates and unreliable sources. From the next
rebuild, the US deck cites those 14. Someone reading "what the US press says" now gets exactly that.

**The UK market had nothing to say.** The UK topic was switched off on 21 September because it
found almost no UK coverage. Our news sources return very little UK oral-health press. The whole
Sunstar collection held 16 usable UK articles, gathered over three and a half months. We added
three UK publications as direct feeds. On their first pass they supplied two on-topic UK
articles, including The Guardian's "The mouth is a gateway into your body".

**The topic list was hard to read.** Three sets of topics cover the same subject from different
sources, and each set used a different name. "Oral-Systemic Health Research" held scientific
papers. "Oral Health & Whole-Body Health – Germany" held German press. "Oral Health – Consumer
Voice" held social media posts. Nothing in those names showed they were one subject. They now
read "Oral-Systemic Health – Research", "– Press, Germany" and "– Consumer Voice", and they sit
together in the list. The people who notice are the analysts who manage topics on the site.

## Release notes (copy-ready)
- Country decks in the Forecast Tracker now cite only publishers based in that country.
- The cross-market "Oral-Systemic Health" deck is unchanged and still draws on every market.
- We added UK dental and health press as direct sources for the UK market.
- Oral-health topics now follow one naming pattern: subject, source type, market.

## Demo / walkthrough
- **Topic names:** open the Topics page. The ten oral-health topics are listed together under
  "Oral-Systemic Health – …".
- **Country filtering:** rebuild a market deck in the Forecast Tracker, for example the United
  States deck, and compare its sources with the previous version. We have not rebuilt any deck
  yet, so no customer-visible deck has changed.

## Positioning notes
A customer who buys a per-market view expects each market to reflect local coverage. Before this
change, the product could not honour that promise. We asked each news source to limit results to
a country, and none of them does it reliably. So we now work out each publisher's country
ourselves and filter on that. The claim we can make is modest: a country deck cites that
country's publishers. We cannot claim to cover all of that country's press. The volumes below
show why.

## Limits and what's next
- **Several market decks are now too thin to rebuild.** Spain, Brazil and the UK each have one
  usable article. France has four and Italy two. The country rule is correct, but it leaves
  these markets without enough evidence for a deck. Someone needs to decide whether to add
  sources for them, merge them, or hold them back. We have not made that call.
- **The UK market is a trial.** We will count usable UK articles in about two weeks. If there are
  around 20, we switch the UK topic back on and build its deck. If not, UK oral-health press is
  too thin to support a deck, and we stop spending on it.
- **Publishers we cannot place are left out.** A market deck drops articles whose publisher's
  country is unknown. This happens most to press releases and aggregator sites.
- **Existing decks are untouched.** We have not rebuilt any deck. The approved cross-market deck
  was not affected.
