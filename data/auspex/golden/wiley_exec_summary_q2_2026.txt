The bottom line. Forecasts anticipated progress in restoring science-policy trust in the U.S., but recent evidence points to the opposite. The Trump administration's revocation of the EPA's endangerment finding and other climate science rollbacks have directly undermined expectations for renewed science-policy integration. Several scenarios in Open Science and Scientific Publishing were expected to advance, such as stronger publishing integrity and open access model transformation, yet no significant policy or legal developments have occurred. Meanwhile, the predicted decline in public trust in expertise and peer review is evident, as high-profile retractions and AI-driven citation scandals continue to emerge.

What happened this quarter. The most significant event was the Trump administration's proposal to reduce NASA science funding by 47% for FY 2027, reinforcing the trend of declining federal R&D investment. In publishing, Northwestern's $2.3 million settlement over falsified research and an increase in paper retractions have intensified scrutiny on research integrity. The patent environment shifted as Novo Nordisk's semaglutide patent expiry led to a surge of low-cost generics in India and price reductions in the U.S. and Canada, with Dr. Reddy's and Natco Pharma expanding their market share. Media coverage of integrity and peer review issues increased, with The Lancet and ArXiv highlighting ongoing vulnerabilities.

The headline tail risk. The leading tail-risk scenario remains the "Collapse of Scientific Consensus Due to Mass Peer Review Delegitimization." If the current trend of public distrust and high-profile retractions continues, the legitimacy of peer review could erode, fragmenting knowledge into competing silos and destabilizing public health and policy decision-making. For Wiley, this would threaten the core value of trusted, peer-reviewed content and could accelerate declines in subscriptions and revenue.

What this means for Wiley. Editorial integrity and trust face intense pressure. Skepticism toward health authorities has grown, as shown by a recent study reporting increasing refusal of newborn preventative care linked to mistrust in scientific expertise. In open access, the slowdown of Diamond OA and ongoing legal barriers, such as OpenClaw's AI sharing restrictions, indicate that sustainable funding and access frameworks remain unresolved. Federal R&D exposure is heightened by the proposed 47% NASA budget cut and MIT's reported declines in research funding and graduate admissions. Internationally, India's rapid adoption of open-access models and China's expanding technology sector are changing global competition and access standards.

Next quarter. The priorities are to strengthen trust mechanisms, prepare for continued policy volatility, and guide responsible technology adoption. Initiatives should focus on rebuilding peer review credibility, publishing clear standards for AI-driven publishing practices, and renegotiating partnerships to diversify revenue streams given U.S. federal cutbacks. Watch for further federal budget decisions, legal developments in open science, and major publisher responses to integrity and trust challenges.
